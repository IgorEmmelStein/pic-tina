# UVP And Positioning

Extract positioning from evidence, not slogans.

## Useful Inputs

- What the client sells
- Who the offer is best for
- What alternative the buyer is comparing against
- What pain or delay the client removes
- What proof supports the promise
- What the buyer gets that is hard for competitors to copy

## Strong UVP Test

A strong UVP should answer:
- Who is this for?
- What outcome do they want?
- What specific mechanism helps them get it?
- Why believe this client can deliver it?

Avoid UVPs built from generic words like quality, best, trusted, experienced, affordable, comprehensive, or customer-focused unless the sources prove a specific version of the claim.

## Positioning Statement Formula

For `[ICP]` who need `[outcome]`, `[client]` is a `[category]` that helps them `[specific result]` through `[proof-backed mechanism]`, unlike `[alternative]` which often creates `[specific friction]`.
