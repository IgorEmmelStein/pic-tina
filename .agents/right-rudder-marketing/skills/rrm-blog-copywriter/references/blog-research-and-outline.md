# Blog Research And Outline

Use this before drafting.

## Inputs To Check

- Canonical brand guide at `.agents/right-rudder-marketing/shared/outputs/brand-guide.md`
- Brand guide message pillars, proof points, safe claims, risky claims, CTAs, and evergreen blog opportunities
- Aviation fact-check report, especially `Topic Context`, approved language, avoid list, and required qualifiers
- Existing blog posts to avoid duplicate intent
- Existing blog post authors, author images, staff/team pages, and byline conventions
- Existing blog post `heroImage` values to avoid reuse
- Program pages and other internal pages that can support section-level backlinks
- SEO exports or Search Console data under `.agents/right-rudder-marketing/extra-information/seo-data/`
- SERP notes or competitor pages if the user provides them
- Web search results only when web access is available or explicitly requested

## Angle Rules

- Do not write a post that only repeats what already ranks.
- Use a specific reader situation, industry mechanism, local factor, cost/timeline risk, or decision tradeoff as the angle.
- Derive the angle from the brand guide, search intent, existing posts, repo proof, and aviation fact-check constraints.
- Make the hook and section path feel human before drafting. A correct outline is not enough if it reads like a source report.
- Turn proof into lived context: show the student scenario, the mechanism, and the consequence.
- Place required qualifiers near the claim they limit, then move forward. Do not repeat disclaimers until the article feels afraid of its own point.
- Convert evidence into final-copy perspective before drafting. Repo pages, fact-check reports, video notes, transcripts, and information dumps should never surface as "the page shows," "listed in the repo," "the episode notes describe," or similar source-audit phrasing.
- Never write "our [program/rentals/fleet] page lists/shows/says" or "our website says" in final copy. Translate the supported fact into direct company offer language, such as "Here at [School], we offer..." or "Our rental options include..."
- Decide how to handle unverified exact details during planning. Omit, generalize, or use one natural contact-us sentence; do not write caveats that make the reader feel like the company is unsure of itself.
- Plan where company voice belongs. For company-specific facts, use `we`, `our`, or `us` after the first clear company reference.
- Plan byline handling. Team bylines use school-staff voice. Named authors get light, sourced persona cues only when the author evidence supports them; otherwise use team voice.
- If the brand guide does not support a distinct angle, ask for a brand guide update instead of creating a tactical blog brief.

## Existing Post Improvement

When improving a draft, identify:
- What is working
- What is missing
- What is hurting search intent match
- What is hurting trust
- Which claims need fact-checking
- Which sections should be rewritten, removed, or expanded
