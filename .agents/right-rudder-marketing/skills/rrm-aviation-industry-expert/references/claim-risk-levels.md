# Claim Risk Levels

Use these levels when reviewing aviation content.

## verified

The claim is supported by the local aviation reference pack or by a user-provided authoritative source. Include the source.

## verified for user-provided context

The claim is supported by the user's information dump, transcript notes, client notes, call notes, or other supplied private context, but is not independently verified as a public fact. Use this for video contents, private event details, client-only facts, and observations the writer needs for the post.

Use attribution when appropriate, such as "in the video," "the client notes say," or "according to the supplied transcript." Do not turn this into a public or evergreen hard fact unless another source supports it.

Keep that attribution in the fact-check report when it is only useful for internal traceability. Do not instruct the blog writer to publish source-audit phrases such as "the supplied transcript says" or "the episode notes describe" unless attribution itself is valuable to the reader.

## needs source

The claim may be true but cannot be verified from available local material. Do not let the writer use it as a hard fact.

## risky

The claim may be partly true but needs qualification because it is time-sensitive, depends on school-specific context, varies by student, or touches regulation, safety, money, or career outcomes.

Risky does not mean unusable. When a useful claim is risky, give a precise rewrite that preserves the truthful mechanism and removes the overreach. Prefer one clean qualifier near the claim over repeated defensive language throughout the post.

## do not use

The claim is unsupported, misleading, framed as a guarantee, or contradicted by available sources.

## Common Risk Triggers

- Guaranteed career outcomes
- Guaranteed checkride or completion timelines
- Superlatives like safest, best, fastest, cheapest
- Universal FAA interpretations without source
- Salary or hiring-demand claims without current sources
- Medical eligibility claims that sound like advice

## Rewrite Standard

- Replace broad guarantees with specific mechanisms.
- Replace vague warnings with exact limits.
- Tell the writer what can be said confidently.
- Tell the writer where the qualifier belongs.
- Note when a verified claim should not be weakened by unnecessary hedging.
- Tell the writer when the final post should omit an unverified exact detail instead of publishing a confirmation caveat.
- Translate internal source language into company-seat wording. For example, "the current fleet page shows" becomes "our fleet currently includes" when supported, and "ask the school how" becomes "contact us to talk through" when a next step is useful.
- Translate company-owned page references too. For example, "our rentals page lists" becomes "our rental options include" or "here at the school, we offer" when supported.
