# Ratings And Certificates

Reference notes for aviation credential terminology.

Reviewed on: 2026-04-29

## Terminology

- A certificate is the FAA credential level, such as student pilot, private pilot, commercial pilot, airline transport pilot, flight instructor, or ground instructor.
- A rating is an authorization placed on a certificate, such as airplane single-engine land, airplane multiengine land, instrument airplane, or a type rating.
- An endorsement is an instructor logbook or training-record authorization for a specific operation, test, aircraft characteristic, solo privilege, or training event. Examples include solo endorsements, complex airplane, high-performance airplane, and tailwheel endorsements.
- A category is a broad aircraft grouping, such as airplane, rotorcraft, glider, lighter-than-air, powered-lift, powered parachute, or weight-shift-control.
- A class is a grouping within a category, such as airplane single-engine land, airplane multiengine land, rotorcraft-helicopter, or lighter-than-air balloon.
- A type rating is required for large aircraft, turbojet-powered airplanes, powered-lift, and other aircraft specified by the Administrator.

## Common Credentials

- Student Pilot Certificate: Used during training and required before solo flight; solo privileges also require instructor endorsements and applicable medical qualification.
- Private Pilot Certificate: Allows noncommercial pilot privileges, including carrying passengers, subject to operating rules, ratings, endorsements, and limitations.
- Instrument Rating: Allows a properly qualified pilot to act as PIC under IFR or in weather below VFR minimums, subject to aircraft equipment, currency, and operating rules.
- Commercial Pilot Certificate: Allows certain compensated or hired operations, but does not automatically authorize every commercial operation or airline job.
- Certified Flight Instructor: A flight instructor certificate, commonly called CFI, allows instruction within the instructor's ratings and limitations.
- CFII: Informal shorthand for a flight instructor with instrument instructor privileges. Prefer "flight instructor instrument" or "CFI-I/CFII" only if the audience will understand it.
- MEI: Informal shorthand for a flight instructor authorized for multiengine instruction. Verify the instructor certificate and airplane multiengine class privileges before using in claims.
- Multi-Engine Rating: Usually a class rating added to a pilot certificate. Do not call it a standalone certificate.
- ATP Certificate: Airline transport pilot certificate. Required for many airline and air carrier roles, with standard and restricted-privilege pathways depending on experience and qualifications.

## Safer Language

- Use: "earn a private pilot certificate."
- Avoid: "earn a private pilot license" in formal FAA-facing copy. "License" is common public language, but FAA sources use "certificate."
- Use: "add an instrument rating."
- Avoid: "get an instrument certificate."
- Use: "add a multi-engine rating to a pilot certificate."
- Avoid: "get a multi-engine license."

## Claim Checks

- Use credential names consistently.
- Define aviation terms in reader-friendly language for prospective students.
- Do not overstate privileges attached to a rating or certificate.
- Verify CFI, CFII, MEI, multi-engine, instrument, and ATP claims against the exact certificate/rating being discussed.
- Client-specific staff credential claims require current client documentation or FAA airman verification.
