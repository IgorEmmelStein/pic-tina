# Part 61 Vs Part 141

Reference notes for Part 61 and Part 141 flight training claims.

Reviewed on: 2026-04-29

## What Part 61 Governs

- 14 CFR Part 61 governs certification of pilots, flight instructors, and ground instructors, including eligibility, aeronautical knowledge, flight proficiency, aeronautical experience, privileges, limitations, practical-test prerequisites, medical certificate rules, and instructor privileges.
- Part 61 training is often described as more flexible because an authorized instructor may train a student outside an FAA-certificated Part 141 school, but the applicant still must meet the applicable Part 61 certificate or rating requirements.
- A school or instructor operating under Part 61 should not be described as "FAA approved" in the same way as a certificated Part 141 pilot school unless there is a specific FAA approval or certificate to support the claim.

## What Part 141 Governs

- 14 CFR Part 141 governs FAA-certificated pilot schools and approved training courses.
- The FAA describes Part 141 schools as using structured training programs and FAA-approved course curricula, with requirements for facilities, equipment, personnel, instructors, course outlines, records, and stage checks.
- A school can claim Part 141 status only if it holds the relevant FAA pilot school certificate and approved courses. Verify the exact courses before saying a program is "Part 141 approved."

## Minimum-Hour And Structure Claims

- Private pilot airplane single-engine under Part 61 requires at least 40 hours of flight time under 14 CFR 61.109, with specific dual, solo, cross-country, night, instrument, and test-preparation requirements.
- Part 141 Appendix B prescribes a private pilot certification course curriculum. For an airplane, rotorcraft, powered-lift, or airship rating, the approved course includes at least 35 hours of flight training, plus required ground training and stage/end-of-course checks.
- Reduced-hour statements must be tied to the specific Part 141 approved course, certificate/rating, category, and class. Do not imply every Part 141 course reduces every requirement.
- Do not say Part 141 is automatically better, faster, cheaper, or more professional than Part 61. The better fit depends on student goals, schedule, funding, learning style, school quality, and available aircraft/instructors.

## Safer Language

- Use: "Part 141 schools operate FAA-approved training courses with a structured syllabus and required records; Part 61 training can offer more scheduling and pacing flexibility."
- Avoid: "Part 141 is always faster and better."
- Use: "Part 141 may allow lower minimum flight-time requirements for some approved courses, but actual completion time varies."
- Avoid: "Part 141 students always finish in fewer hours."

## Claim Checks

- Verify exact hour claims before use.
- Do not imply Part 141 is automatically better for every student.
- Qualify reduced-hour claims by certificate, rating, aircraft category/class, and approved course.
- Client claims about being Part 141, having VA eligibility, having specific approved courses, or offering financing through a Part 141 program require current client or FAA school documentation.
