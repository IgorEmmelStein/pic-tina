# Checkrides And DPEs

Reference notes for checkride and Designated Pilot Examiner claims.

Reviewed on: 2026-04-29

## What A Checkride Is

- A checkride is the practical test for an FAA certificate or rating. It commonly includes an oral portion and a flight portion, evaluated against the applicable FAA Airman Certification Standards or Practical Test Standards.
- The FAA practical test rules are in 14 CFR 61.39, 61.43, 61.45, 61.47, and 61.49.
- A practical-test applicant generally needs to meet eligibility, training, aeronautical experience, knowledge-test, instructor endorsement, aircraft, and document requirements before testing.
- The FAA private pilot practical-test FAQ says the applicant must provide an airworthy aircraft with equipment relevant to the test and present aircraft documents, application, knowledge-test report if applicable, medical certificate when required, pilot certificate, logbook, and graduation certificate if applicable.

## Designated Pilot Examiners

- A Designated Pilot Examiner, or DPE, is an FAA-appointed designee under 14 CFR 183.23 who may conduct practical tests within the authority of the designation.
- FAA says a DPE must hold appropriate category, class, and, if appropriate, type ratings. Designation is a privilege and may be rescinded by the FAA.
- DPEs are not interchangeable for every test. Their authority depends on designation, aircraft, rating, location, and FAA designee management rules.
- Use the FAA Designee Locator or current client-provided scheduling information for DPE availability claims.

## ACS And Standards

- FAA Airman Certification Standards define the knowledge, risk management, and skill elements for many pilot practical tests.
- The FAA ACS page should be checked before making current test-standard claims. As reviewed on 2026-04-29, the FAA lists current airplane private, instrument, commercial, ATP, and flight instructor ACS documents with effective dates.
- Do not rely on old "PTS" wording for tests that have moved to ACS unless the FAA still lists a PTS for that certificate/rating.

## Safer Language

- Use: "Your instructor recommends you for the practical test when you meet the applicable requirements and are ready."
- Avoid: "We guarantee you will pass your checkride."
- Use: "DPE availability varies by location, examiner authorization, aircraft, scheduling demand, and FAA oversight."
- Avoid: "We can always get you a checkride next week."

## Claim Checks

- Client-specific DPE access must come from client sources.
- Avoid universal wait-time claims unless sourced with current local data.
- Do not imply a checkride outcome is guaranteed.
- Treat pass-rate, first-time-pass, "easiest DPE," or "fastest checkride" claims as high risk.
- Verify whether a claim refers to an FAA inspector, DPE, check airman, training center evaluator, or school stage-check instructor.
